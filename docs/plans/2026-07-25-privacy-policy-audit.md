# Privacy Policy Audit — 2026-07-25

Full recheck of `privacy/` (privacy policy EN/FR, terms of service EN/FR) against the codebase,
run as a 19-agent Opus workflow (9 audit dimensions → adversarial verification → completeness critic).
The policies were last updated 2026-04-02; 287 commits landed since.

**Outcome: 89 confirmed discrepancies, 18 findings refuted by verification, 28 claims verified accurate.**
All confirmed findings were applied to the policy files on 2026-07-25 (see git diff of `privacy/`).
Open factual questions are embedded in the policy sources as HTML comments (`<!-- TODO(owner): ... -->`,
invisible when rendered) and consolidated in the "Open questions" section below.

## Headline issues

1. **"Permanent deletion within 30 days" was false** — account deletion only sets `deleted=true`
   (`backend/.../service/user/UserService.java:75`); no purge job exists. Policy now describes
   deactivation + manual erasure on request.
2. **Strava sign-in entirely undisclosed** — athlete ID, placeholder emails (`strava_<id>@…`),
   OAuth states/hand-off codes, US transfer (§1, §2, §4, §5 updated).
3. **Biketeam import undisclosed** — accounts, names and social identifiers imported from the
   predecessor platform, incl. members without email (GDPR Art. 14 notice question flagged).
4. **Map tiles, Nominatim geocoding, Esri (US), Google Fonts (mobile, US)** — browser/app-side
   third-party requests contradicted "we do not collect data from third parties" / "data remains in the EU".
5. **Nightly DB+files backups** to a separate host (30 copies) undisclosed.
6. **GPX analysis tool** — uploads retained 30 days, link-is-the-key sharing, public Open Graph map
   thumbnails — all undisclosed.
7. **Rate-limiting and "irreversible hashes" claims overstated** — only OTP issuance is throttled;
   calendar tokens and device pairing codes are stored in cleartext.
8. **Client-side storage wrong for mobile** (Keychain/Keystore, not a cookie; image cache) and
   **absent for Karoo/Garmin** (unencrypted on-device token storage).

## Refuted findings (verified as non-issues — no change made)

- **(privacy-policy §6 Data Retention — "Login sessions | 30 days after last use" (line 142))** Login sessions are retained for 30 days after last use.
  - Refutation: The code facts are right but the conclusion is backwards, so the finding overstates. expiresAt is fixed at creation and never extended, therefore actual retention (30 days from sign-in) is always LESS THAN OR EQUAL TO the documented "30 days after last use" — the policy states a conservative upper bound the implementation respects, not an understatement of retention. Revoked (logged-out) sessions are deleted at the next 03:00 run, i.e. far inside the 30-day window a "use" already licenses. The only excess is the sub-24 h purge lag for a session never used after sign-in, which is immaterial and not user-adverse. §1 line 27 ("up to 30 days") is likewise consistent. Wording could be tightened, but this is not an inaccuracy worth a policy correction.

- **(privacy-policy §4 Technical Service Providers / §5 International Data Transfers ("OVHcloud … All data" / "Our servers are hosted by OVHcloud … Your data remains within the European Union"))** All data lives on OVHcloud servers in France.

- **(privacy-policy §1 "Data Stored Locally on Your Device" + §8 Cookies and Local Storage)** The list of locally stored items is exhaustive for the mobile app.
  - Refutation: Refuted on evidence quality. The only support offered for an on-disk tile cache is that the symbol OfflineManager appears in the package's export list and in its CHANGELOG — the auditor never located a cache being created, sized, or written, and the suggested policy text itself carries a TODO admitting the size limit and expiry are unknown. I searched maplibre 0.3.5: OfflineManager appears only in the barrel export and CHANGELOG, nowhere in lib/src, so the cited lines establish nothing about default runtime behaviour, let alone about this app. The auditor also concedes the cache "contains map imagery only — no information about you", so even if a tile cache exists it holds no personal data and triggers no disclosure duty. A privacy policy should not be amended on a speculative mechanism with a self-admitted unknown.

- **(privacy-policy §9 Security — bullet 3 "Secret hashing" (word "irreversible"))** The stored hashes are irreversible.
  - Refutation: Refuted: the claim overstates and one of its two pillars is factually wrong. (a) The 6-character device pairing code cannot be 'recovered from the stored hash' because it is not hashed at all — DeviceCode.java:30-31 stores `user_code` in cleartext; the value that IS hashed for devices is a 32-byte random secret (DeviceAuthService.java:267-271, 282-290), for which SHA-256 is entirely adequate. That leaves only the 6-digit OTP. (b) For the OTP, the weakness is the entropy of the input, not the hash function: SHA-256 is a one-way function, and the plain-language statement 'stored as irreversible hashes' communicates the accurate fact that the original value is not kept in readable form. The policy nowhere promises salting, key-stretching or resistance to an attacker who already holds the database. The OTP is additionally single-use (AuthToken.isValid checks usedAt, AuthToken.java:81-84), invalidated on issue of a new one (AuthService.java:249-250) and lives 5 minutes (:262), matching §1:28 and §6:143. This is a hardening suggestion, not a documentation/code discrepancy.

- **(privacy-policy §9 Security — bullet 5 "Multi-tenant isolation")** Each domain's data is strictly isolated at the database level.
  - Refutation: Facts check out but the claim does not survive as a discrepancy. I confirmed there is no RLS (no 'ROW LEVEL SECURITY'/'CREATE POLICY' in any of the 26 migrations in backend/src/main/resources/db/migration/) and a single shared schema. However, the substantive promise — strict isolation of each domain's data — holds: the domain predicate is injected unconditionally into every generated query at TeamEntityRepository.java:199-204 ('te.team.domain.id = :domainId'), the domain comes from the request host (DomainResolver) and the two lookups the auditor calls 'bypasses' explicitly re-verify the tenant before returning anything (AuthService.java:429-432 throws ForbiddenException on domain mismatch; DeviceAuthService.java:129-132 throws TOKEN_INVALID). No cross-domain leak path was found. 'At the database level' is ambiguous wording that is arguably satisfied by 'enforced in every database query', and no data-subject-relevant fact is misstated. Tightening the sentence is a legitimate editorial improvement, not a confirmed inaccuracy.

"You must be at least 16 years old" is a contractual eligibility condition imposed on the user, not an assertion that the service measures age; a terms document is the normal and sufficient place to impose it, and no clause claims verification. Privacy-policy §10 is already drafted to match the implementation exactly: it says Pedalons "is not intended for" under-16s and that "We do not **knowingly** collect" their data — the "knowingly" qualifier is precisely the wording used when there is no age gate, and it invites parents to report. There is therefore no discrepancy between the documents and the code; the suggested checkbox is a product hardening idea, not a correction.

- **(terms §1 Acceptance of Terms / §3 Registration and Account)** "By using Pedalons, you agree to these terms of service." — implying the terms are put in front of users when they commit to the service.
  - Refutation: Refuted. The claim rests entirely on the auditor's own gloss ("implying the terms are put in front of users"), which §1 does not say. §1 states that acceptance follows from use — a browsewrap formulation — and that is exactly what the code implements: no checkbox, no gate, acceptance by use, with the documents reachable from the login screen (web) and from the profile screen (both platforms). The document accurately describes the mechanism it relies on, so there is no doc-vs-code discrepancy. The underlying observation is real and worth a product ticket (the web register branch and both mobile auth branches omit the notice that the login branch shows), and the enforceability of browsewrap acceptance under EU consumer law is a legal-quality concern — but neither makes §1 inaccurate.

- **(terms §8 Termination)** "You may delete your account at any time" — read as available wherever you use Pedalons (the same terms are bundled and displayed inside the mobile app).
  - Refutation: Refuted as an overreading.
